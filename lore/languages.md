# Fragmenta — Ancient Languages Reference: Structured Digest

Source: *Ancient Languages of the World — A Reference Compendium (Expanded Edition)*, canonical language reference for Fragmenta. Covers two languages: **Vaur'eth** and **Keth-Rakar**. The Fragmenta Bestiary draws creature classification names directly from these two languages (cross-reference at end of source doc). New words are meant to be coined only in a "designated working chat" to preserve phonological consistency — i.e., these are living, extensible conlangs, not closed lists.

---

## 1. Languages/Dialects Overview

### Vaur'eth — "Ancient Druidic Tongue of the Deep Swamp"
- **Status:** Dead/ancient but *inherited* — no longer a language of courts or scholars, but still spoken in fragments by certain living characters (a "living inheritance").
- **Age:** The oldest language on the continent — predates the nations, the Great Mage War, and written history.
- **Origin/character:** Grew organically from the swamp; spoken by people who lived close enough to the natural world that "the natural world talked back." Not a language of civilization/record — a language of breath, closeness to nature, and quiet utterance in deep places.
- **Sacred/common:** Folk/animist register rather than formal-sacred, though it carries ritual and reverent uses (see Druid Speech Pattern Guide). Associated closely with druidic and primal/nature magic.
- **Notable bearer:** "The Great Lizard" carries Vaur'eth in its bones — described as a language "not remembered but inhabited." Also the ancient druid berserker Voraul/Vraul (see Proper Names).
- **Register note:** A fluent Vaur'eth speaker would find Keth-Rakar totally foreign, and vice versa — the two share no root and developed independently.

### Keth-Rakar — "Old Tongue of Dar-Rakvantim — The Carved Language of Thraekor"
- **Status:** Dead/ancient — the language of the dwarven-dragonborn civilization (Dar-Rakvantim) that *preceded* Thraekor.
- **Origin/character:** Built for permanence, not conversation — designed to be carved into stone walls, stamped into metal, inscribed in oath-chambers. Short, hard words that "survive being chiseled." A language of record, oath, law, and monument rather than daily speech.
- **Sacred/formal:** Used for oath-chambers, tomb walls, ritual carvings, and weapon inscriptions. The altar beneath **Khar-Athel** has documentation written in this script.
- **Tone:** Deliberately the phonological and cultural opposite of Vaur'eth — "lands like stone" vs. Vaur'eth's breathing/flowing quality.

### Contact-Era Compounds (not a third language, but a designated hybrid category)
- A historical period ("Dar-Rakvantim period") when dwarven and swamp-adjacent cultures had contact at their territorial borders, producing blended words combining roots from both languages for concepts neither language alone could name (e.g., animated metal).
- This is explicitly called out as **the only sanctioned blending** of the two languages — important rule for consistency when coining new names.
- Examples: **Skarr-Daun** (iron + life = "iron given life"), **Sul-Keth** (unlife-negation + stone = "the wrong-living bone / stone that should not move").

## 2. Naming Conventions & Sound Patterns (for generative name-building)

### Vaur'eth — phonological profile
- **Consonants:** V, N, R, S, W, M, L, Th (soft, as in "the"), Dh (voiced th), Kh (guttural)
- **Vowels:** au (as in dawn), ae (as in day), o, ua (two-ah), ei (as in grey)
- **Word-ending rule:** Words typically end in a vowel, or in -n / -r / -l.
- **Stress:** Falls on the first syllable.
- **No double consonants** — "this language breathes between sounds."
- **Overall feel:** soft, open, breath-shaped, meant to be spoken quietly.

**Grammar / morphology patterns (usable as generative suffix/prefix rules):**
- Plural: suffix **-aun** → voran (blood) → voranaun (bloods/many lives)
- Negation: prefix **sul-** → daun (life) → sul-daun (unlife/corrupted living)
- Belonging/'of': suffix **-or** → vaur (root) → vauror (of the root/rooted)
- Intensifier ('true'/'final'): prefix **rau-** → mael (death) → rau-mael (true death)
- Compounding: two roots joined directly → nori + voran = nori-voran (fire-blood/passion-kin)
- Persistence suffix **-uin** ("one bound to / those who persist in"): Orvae (memory) + -uin = Orvuin (the memory-bound/ghost); Voran (blood) + -uin = Voruin (kin)

### Keth-Rakar — phonological profile
- **Consonants:** K, G, D, R, Th, V, Z, Sk, Kr, Dr, Gh
- **Vowels:** a (flat), e (short), i (short) — **no long vowels, no diphthongs**
- **Word-ending rule:** Words end on consonants more often than vowels.
- **Onset clusters:** Consonant clusters at the start are common: Skr-, Drak-, Kreth-, Gh-.
- **Overall feel:** hard, short, made to be carved rather than spoken softly.

**Grammar / morphology patterns:**
- Plural: suffix **-ak** → keth (stone) → kethak (stones)
- Negation: prefix **veth-** → akem (oath) → vethakem (broken oath/betrayal)
- Belonging/'of': suffix **-im** → keth (stone) → kethim (of stone/stone's own)
- Completed past: prefix **drak-** → threk (war) → drakthrek (the war that was fought)
- Compounding: two roots hyphenated → keth + akem = keth-akem (stone-oath/the carved promise)

### Design notes for coining new words (from the doc's own guidance)
- "Vaur'eth words should feel like they could end in breath. Keth-Rakar words should feel like they could end in stone."
- The two languages are intentionally built to sound nothing alike — preserve that contrast when generating new names in each.
- New Vaur'eth/Keth-Rakar coinages should be checked against the existing vocabulary lists (below) before being treated as canon, per the doc's own bestiary cross-reference practice.
- Contact-era hybrid words should be rare/special-cased — reserved for concepts that legitimately span both cultures (per the doc, historically at the Dar-Rakvantim cultural border).

### Observed micro-patterns worth reusing generatively
- Vaur'eth roots recombine freely via simple concatenation/hyphenation (nori-voran, sael sueln, awen veth, etc.) — good model for compound place/NPC names.
- Keth-Rakar shows deliberate "near-miss" pairs that share an opening but diverge in meaning by design (documented explicitly in the source as intentional, not errors):
  - *Wovael* (to ambush) vs. *Worvael* (to cry out) — share a **Wo-** opening.
  - *Ghravek* (jaw) vs. *Grathek* (maw) — related but phonetically distinct, different scale of meaning.
  - *Krathdim* (to besiege) vs. *Ghrekdim* (to crush) — share a **-dim** ending but differ in onset.
  - This "family resemblance without collision" pattern is explicitly called a design standard for Keth-Rakar and is worth mimicking when generating new related terms (e.g., creature names in the same family).

**IV. Druid Character — Speech Pattern Guide**
> He uses Vaur'eth words where Common fails him — not to show off the language but because the concept doesn't translate. His speech is broken Common threaded with single Vaur'eth words or short pairs. He never speaks in full Vaur'eth sentences unless in ritual or vision contexts.

## 4. Writing Systems / Scripts

The source document does **not** contain a dedicated alphabet, rune set, or glyph-by-glyph script description for either language. What it does establish:

- **Keth-Rakar has an actual carved/inscribed script** — described only functionally, not visually: "carved into stone walls, stamped into metal, inscribed in oath-chambers... designed for record... its short, hard words survive being chiseled." The altar beneath **Khar-Athel** has documentation written in this script.
- **Vaur'eth is explicitly NOT a written/scholarly language** — it exists as "carved inscriptions, spoken utterances from ancient figures, and the broken speech of characters who carry these tongues" — implying any Vaur'eth "writing" that exists is closer to sparse ritual inscription than a full script, consistent with its oral/breath-based nature.
- No rune names, letterforms, or symbol-to-sound mappings are given anywhere in the document (confirmed via full-text search for "rune," "glyph," "alphabet," "script," "symbol").

If the game needs an actual glyph system, this is an open gap the reference doesn't fill — worth flagging to the user if visual runes/writing are needed for props, inscriptions, or UI flavor.

## 5. Lore Tied to the Languages

- **Vaur'eth and the Great Lizard:** Vaur'eth is "the language carried in the bones of the Great Lizard — not remembered but inhabited." This creature is the living vessel/carrier of the language, not merely a speaker of it.
- **Keth-Rakar, Dar-Rakvantim, and Thraekor:** Keth-Rakar is "the Old Tongue of Dar-Rakvantim" and "the Carved Language of Thraekor" — the language of the dwarven-dragonborn civilization that *preceded* Thraekor (Thraekor appears to be a successor state/civilization/faction). "Velth skrel" (Ash endures) is called "the Thraekor core principle," suggesting Thraekor inherited/adopted Keth-Rakar values even if it's a later culture.
- **Khar-Athel:** An altar beneath Khar-Athel has documentation carved in Keth-Rakar — ties this location into the dwarven-dragonborn civilization's religious/record-keeping practice.
- **The Great Mage War:** Referenced only as a historical anchor point — Vaur'eth predates it. No further detail given in this document.
- **Voraul/Vraul:** An ancient druid berserker whose full ceremonial Vaur'eth name (Voraul, "blood-ember") compressed over time into the name everyone actually uses (Vraul) — an in-world example of language drift/oral history in action, and a reusable template for other legendary NPCs.
- **Contact-era compounds & Dar-Rakvantim period:** Historical linguistic contact zone between swamp-adjacent (Vaur'eth) and dwarven-dragonborn (Keth-Rakar) cultures produced sanctioned hybrid vocabulary (Skarr-Daun, Sul-Keth) for concepts neither culture had alone — this is presented as *the only* legitimate blending point between the two language families, useful as a lore-consistent naming rule (don't casually mix roots outside this designated contact-era context).
- **Bestiary linkage:** The Fragmenta Bestiary's creature classification names are explicitly built from these two languages' roots (plus contact-era compounds), e.g.:
  - Draven-Drak (Keth-Rakar: ancestor + fire)
  - Drakenvalth (Keth-Rakar: fire + linking form + coined "vault/great form" — "Norrvael designation")

  - Thueln-Maur (Vaur'eth: stone/permanence + movement)

  - This gives a direct, reusable pattern for generating new creature/place/item names: pick 2 roots (same language, or contact-era if genuinely crossing the two cultures), hyphenate or compress per that language's grammar rules, and cross-check against the existing list to avoid collisions.
- **General cross-language principle (explicit design intent):** "These two languages share no root and developed independently. They sound nothing alike — which is intentional... A character fluent in one would find the other entirely foreign." This is a hard constraint for consistency: never let Vaur'eth and Keth-Rakar naming conventions blur except in the deliberately-flagged contact-era category.

## Quick-Reference Cheat Sheet for Procedural Name Generation

**Vaur'eth (nature/druidic/dead-but-inherited, oral, sacred-in-a-folk-sense):**
- Consonants: V N R S W M L Th Dh Kh — Vowels: au ae o ua ei
- End words in a vowel or -n/-r/-l; stress first syllable; never double consonants
- Productive affixes: -aun (plural), sul- (negation), -or (belonging), rau- (true/final), -uin (persistence/"bound to")
- Compound by direct concatenation or hyphenation of two roots

**Keth-Rakar (dwarven-dragonborn/dead, carved/monumental, record-and-oath register):**
- Consonants: K G D R Th V Z Sk Kr Dr Gh — Vowels: a e i only (short, no diphthongs)
- End words on consonants; common onset clusters Skr- Drak- Kreth- Gh-
- Productive affixes: -ak (plural), veth- (negation), -im (belonging), drak- (completed past)
- Compound via hyphenation of two roots

**Contact-era (rare, deliberate hybrid):** one Vaur'eth root + one Keth-Rakar root, only for concepts spanning both cultures historically (e.g., animate metal, unliving stone).
