# Fragmenta Bestiary Digest — Chapter 13 (Compiled Volume: "A Naturalist's Compendium of Magical Creatures")

**Source:** Compiled by Davin the Archivist DeVerre, Retired, Veil Division, Gods' Hand Kabal, Contracted Researcher, Mugamiir Safor. The volume is organized into three sub-chapters: **I. Solitaries**, **II. The Unnatural**, **III. Continentals**. Regions referenced throughout: Vaeloris, Sahrimor, Thraekor, Norrvael, Sanguivorum (the five nations), plus Drath Vorrumbruk (lizardfolk swamp/ocean-border territory) and the Black Sands (Sahrimor). Institutional references: the Gods' Hand Kabal (and its Veil Division), the Fist of the Hand (Martialum-trained combat mages), the Vitae school, "Bruised" mages (those who work outside sanctioned magic, killed on sight). Magic "paths" referenced: Study, Nature, Faith.

## I. SOLITARIES
*Creatures existing in such small numbers or singular circumstance that population/distribution language doesn't apply. One exception (the Vaelorn) heals rather than harms.*

### Ancient Dragons (Drakenvalth)
- **Name:** Ancient Dragons — Norrvael Mythological Designation: **Drakenvalth**. Classification: Unverified. Hypothetical Apex.
- **Description/Appearance:** Never confirmed to exist by Davin; hypothesized as categorically beyond dragons ("the ocean is not simply a large river" compared to Skrel-Drak/Draven-Drak). No physical description available — purely inferential.
- **Habitat:** Unknown, potentially continental.
- **Behavior/Threat:** Threat assessment "Indeterminate. Possibly without ceiling." Hypothesized as near-infinite in power, entirely self-directed, answering to nothing and no one. Historical record (unconfirmed) suggests when its will "has turned toward the affairs of civilisation, the results have not been small."
- **Lore/Origin:** Extrapolated hierarchy: Skrel-Drak (Drake) → Draven-Drak (Dragon) → Drakenvalth. Referenced in 11 separate accounts across four unconnected civilizations in the Kabal's restricted historical archive. Skrel-Drak exhibit an unexplained "directionality" — an instinctive pull toward something above them — which Davin takes as circumstantial evidence for the Drakenvalth's existence.

### Draven-Drak
- **Name:** Draven-Drak — Common Tongue: **Dragon**. Naturalist Classification: Draven-Drak — "Ancestor-fire, the fire of those before."
- **Description/Appearance:** Categorically larger than Skrel-Drak; size varies hugely by individual (smallest observed: white-scaled female, coastal cliffs of western Sanguivorum, ~4x a large Skrel-Drak's mass; largest: red-scaled male, volcanic ranges of Thraekor, ~8-9x that mass). Appears to grow continuously across a lifespan (similar to elven physiology). Dense, layered scales with faint luminescence (esp. dorsal ridge, wing-membrane joints). Wingspan proportional to (and beyond) body mass — flight "should not be aerodynamically possible."
- **Habitat:** Continental wanderer, ranges freely across borders/oceans/mountains. Loose correlation between scale color and biome: red = volcanic terrain/underground caverns/heat; white = coastal (cliffs, sea stacks, open ocean); blue = high altitude/cold north (reported, not observed); green = dense forest; black = least defined preference (swamp, desert, plain).
- **Behavior/Threat/Combat:** Threat Assessment: Extreme. Encounter Protocol: Withdrawal if possible; engagement not recommended. Carnivorous; large ungulates, cetaceans (coastal specimens), livestock (opportunistic, not aggression-driven). Only 4 credible successful combat engagements documented ever (2 involved coordinated military + mage support). Highly intelligent — 3 of 5 specimens Davin observed showed extraordinary animal intelligence; 2 showed apparent sapience. Abilities/breath by scale color: Red = superheated focused flame or pressurized superheated mist (one unverified account of "lava breath"); White = lightning breath; Black = acidic/expulsion-type breath; Green = healing breath (Davin witnessed a gash close after the dragon breathed on its own tail — unconfirmed causation); Blue = frost/ice breath (reported only); Gold/earth-toned = no breath weapon — instead produce a structured, deliberate **sound/vocalization** with physical effects on air, stone, or soil; Purple = single uncorroborated account, unconfirmed. Davin's theory: the vocalization may be the root mechanism underlying ALL Draven-Drak abilities, with breath simply masking it in most specimens.
- **Lore/Origin:** Reproduction poorly understood — inferred to hatch from eggs (never observed); no evidence of pair bonding or communal nesting. Extraordinarily long-lived: a white female referenced in 11 generations of a coastal settlement's oral history; a red male matched a Keth-Rakar inscription dated ~400 years prior. Davin encountered 5 in his career (white female Sanguivorum, red male Thraekor, black specimen Sahrimor, green female Vaeloris, gold dragon Vaeloris — the gold one "revised my understanding of language").

### Sul-Voran
- **Name:** Sul-Voran — Common Tongue: **Vampire**. Naturalist Classification: Sul-Voran — "Corrupted Blood." Tier designations: The Turned / The Lesser / The Full-Blood / The Elder.
- **Description/Appearance:** Not a species — a condition: a necromantic pathogen (a symbiotic microorganism, deliberately magically engineered) that infects a living host across 4 stages. Full-Blood form: bipedal, taller than any human, architecturally lean frame, elongated limbs/arms hanging "slightly wrong," subtle forward spinal curve. Elongated jaw/snout with fully shifted dentition (not just canines). Fully reflective eyes (no visible whites), amber/pale-gold luminescence in darkness. Grey pallor skin with dark visible veining. Membrane wings from upper back along arms (good takeoff/gliding, NOT sustained long-distance flight). Clawed hands that remain functional.
- **Habitat:** Favors shelter from direct sunlight — dense woodland (most common, all tiers) or caves. In Sahrimor: canyon/cave structures in the desert interior. In Thraekor: abandoned mine/cavern systems. Territorial — flight limitations + pack structure keep them anchored near their turning site (a natural "quarantine").
- **Behavior/Threat/Combat:** Threat variable by tier: Turned = Manageable; Lesser = Dangerous; Full-Blood = Extreme; Elder = Catastrophic. **The Turned**: looks/feels fully human; irritable, territorial, light-sensitive, growing blood-awareness; duration 1 week (severe bite to major vessel) to 4 months (slowest documented). **The Lesser**: feral, tactically dangerous but no restraint/planning; drawn to others of its kind, subordinate to a Full-Blood. **The Full-Blood**: return of rational thought (planning, cause/effect); exercises unconscious mental compulsion over Lessers (wolf-pack-like hierarchy); packs cap around 10-12 before food scarcity causes collapse. **The Elder**: full restoration of original personality/memory/skills; can cast illusions (limited by physical mass — close contact breaks the illusion). Blood mechanics: blood sustains/accelerates healing; human blood most potent; animal blood = slower healing, more erratic (esp. Lessers). Light aversion is a strong preference, NOT a critical weakness — a Sul-Voran in daylight is uncomfortable/irritable but not weak. Infection mechanic: proximity alone ~1/50 chance; claw scratch 30-50%; bite 50-70%; multiple bites in one encounter = effective certainty. Treatment window: 48 hours (extended sunlight exposure, blessed water on wounds, or Faith-path healing magic — highest success rate — after which infection is irreversible). Encounter Protocol: Turned = contain/treat within 48h; Lesser = never engage alone, ranged + eliminate flight first; Full-Blood = coordinated response with mage support, assume a pack; Elder = evacuate, alert the Kabal.
- **Lore/Origin:** Origin of the pathogen itself unknown — Davin suspects deliberate engineering given its staged sophistication ("Someone made this"). Historical incident ~100 years after the Great Mage War: an Elder spent ~1 month deliberately spreading the pathogen through a city in Sanguivorum; the Kabal intervened, purged and burned the city, and struck its name from public record. Davin has confirmed Sul-Voran presence 8 of 11 times investigated; encountered Lessers 4 times, one Full-Blood, and the aftermath of one Elder outbreak (spent 3 undocumented weeks there).

### Zeth-Valdrim
- **Name:** Zeth-Valdrim — Common Tongue: **Leviathan**. Naturalist Classification: Zeth-Valdrim — "Darkness of the Deep."
- **Description/Appearance:** Serpentine/eel-like; moves "like something the water is doing." Smooth body from neck to tail (tail never observed by anyone, per Davin). Translucent skin over jet-black flesh with unpredictable bioluminescent points of light (occasionally blue/green, once an unnameable color). Elongated triangular, near-featureless closed head; small-relative eyes suited to total darkness; sensory picture assembled by unknown non-visual means. Mouth opens far wider than the closed profile suggests, revealing multiple rows of curved, inward-angled, jagged teeth.
- **Habitat:** Deep ocean; documented hunting grounds in Norrvael coastal waters (charted by Norrvael maritime authorities).
- **Behavior/Threat/Combat:** Threat Assessment: Catastrophic to vessels, Extreme — avoid confirmed hunting grounds entirely. Lives at/near ocean floor. Attacks ships by approaching from below (likely misidentifying hulls as large fish due to poor eyesight), bites through the hull, forces its anterior body through the breach "in the manner of an eel," and methodically (not frenziedly) consumes the contents. No observed need to breathe/surface; no visible gill structures. No reliable engagement protocol — recommendation is a lifeboat, not combat.
- **Lore/Origin:** No confirmed magical properties, though the bioluminescent display doesn't map cleanly to known deep-sea bioluminescence mechanisms. Growth data: first sighting (Davin age 23) estimated 120-150 ft length, 40-50 ft cross-section; second sighting (50 years later, same Norrvael waters) estimated 180-220 ft length, 60-70 ft cross-section — roughly 50% size increase over 50 years, growth ceiling unknown.

### Valdrek-Keth — Stage 3 (Diamond Tail)
- **Name:** Valdrek-Keth Stage 3 — Common Tongue/Thraekor Designation: **Diamond Tail**. Naturalist Classification: Valdrek-Keth Stage 3 — "Lava-Stone, Final Form." (Stages 1 & 2 documented in the Thraekor regional section, not in this excerpt.)
- **Description/Appearance:** A living geological formation — the rock shell begun in Stage 1 has fused inseparably with the creature's original adhesive skin ("The skin is the rock. The rock is the skin"). Largest confirmed specimen: footprint/height of a substantial house. Four legs are thick columns of compressed volcanic rock. Defining feature is the tail, tipped in a rock formation the creature deliberately wields as a weapon (cave walls collapse, terrain flattens on impact; a direct human hit is fatal).
- **Habitat:** Region: Thraekor. Active volcanic mountain ranges, deep caldera environments. Absolute territorial instinct — no documented case of two Stage 3 specimens sharing a location.
- **Behavior/Threat/Combat:** Threat: Extreme; engagement without coordinated military and mage support "not recommended under any circumstances." Not an active hunter — primarily geological feeder (rock, mineral deposits, volcanic material); defends territory absolutely. Digs extensively/deeply — documented cases of digging breaking into magma chambers and creating new volcanic activity (a threat to cities above, purely as geological event, not intentional aggression). Interior gemstone deposits (diamonds form from sustained pressure/heat/carbon) concentrate in tail, underbelly, and forehead plating. Terminal-phase individuals crawl into lava flow/magma chamber and don't emerge, rendering deposits permanently inaccessible — hunts are timed to late-life decline. Davin witnessed a coalition of two Thraekor dwarven clans + a Fist of the Hand detachment conduct only the 4th confirmed successful Stage 3 hunt in Thraekor history: took most of a day, cost 11 dead, yielded enough wealth to make every survivor independently wealthy.
- **Lore/Origin:** Nests in dormant/low-activity volcanic zones; up to 200 biological (non-mineralized) eggs per nest, size of a large fruit up to twice that; hatchlings small, fast, not immediately dangerous. Thraekor dwarves named it for what it yields (diamonds), not what it is — reflecting their "assessment, not wonder" relationship to it.

### Orvuin
- **Name:** Orvuin — Common Tongue: **Banshee**. Naturalist Classification: Orvuin — "The Remembered."
- **Description/Appearance:** True self = a calcified magical stone (~size of a human heart) floating at chest-level, containing the compressed spiritual residue of every soul that died on the battlefield where it formed. Projects an ethereal, translucent, cold grey-white luminous humanoid "spirit-cloak" drawn from that residue — can appear human, elven, dwarven, dragonborn, or any species that died there, rendered with perfect fidelity but no memory/recognition/emotion behind the face. When two/three essences are equally dominant, the form flickers between identities (reported as more disturbing than a stable form).
- **Habitat:** Abandoned battlefields where large numbers died violently and were left unburied. Confirmed sightings in the Black Sands of Sahrimor. Does not leave its battlefield.
- **Behavior/Threat/Combat:** Threat: Extreme, uniquely dangerous; conventional combat entirely ineffective; auditory exposure alone constitutes a threat. **The Cry**: merely hearing it establishes an inescapable tether — victims carry it home and are hunted in nightmares; if the Orvuin "finds" the victim in a dream before they wake, it kills them in their sleep (heart absent, undamaged body, four-and-one finger drag marks). Ends only when the Orvuin forgets the victim (moves to more present prey) or the stone is destroyed (severs tether immediately). Hunting behavior: patient ambush predator, approaches always from behind, moves through solid matter, embrace cannot be physically blocked/broken, removes and consumes the soul whole (body undamaged, "it simply stops"). Incomplete/partial contact shortens remaining lifespan proportionally. Most active after midnight, before dawn. Physical death signature identical every time: heart absent, four-and-one finger drag marks inward-then-withdrawn — likely misattributed historically to natural death/heart failure, so true toll is probably undercounted. Not a guardian spirit despite folklore — it hunts the most available prey. Destruction requires physically reaching the stone through the spirit-form or destroying it at range with sufficient magical force; conventional weapons ineffective. Fewer than a dozen credible successful eliminations on record.
- **Lore/Origin:** Correlates with (unconfirmed by Davin as theology) the dominant religious teaching that souls pass into one of the twelve rivers via proper burial rites — the Orvuin manifests exactly where that transition would be incomplete. Davin encountered two in his career; one expedition companion died 14 months after hearing the cry in the Black Sands, heart absent, marks present, age 31.

### Maur-Rau
- **Name:** Maur-Rau (name coined by Davin himself — no established name exists). Regional names: the Armoured One (Thraekor), the Ancient Flyer (Sanguivorum), the Sky Relic (Norrvael maritime accounts). Naturalist Classification: Maur-Rau — "Movement-Sky."
- **Description/Appearance:** Largest known living flying creature. Pterosaur-like frame; long crested head with ten horns arranged in a ring plus a single prominent forehorn; long neck; body seemingly too small for its wings. Dense, owl-like, sound-eliminating feathering, currently silvery-white (shed feathers are dark brown shading to black at the base — Davin believes it was originally jet black, an effective stealth adaptation now lost to age). Wears fitted **armour** over chest, upper neck, wing joints, and partial facial guard around the forehorn/crown — of unidentifiable civilizational origin, fused into the body in places by centuries of feather growth.
- **Habitat:** Continental — circumnavigates the entire known world continuously in a predictable, mappable pattern (window of several weeks per region). Possible (unconfirmed) resting site in northern mountain ranges.
- **Behavior/Threat/Combat:** Threat: Low unless directly beneath it at feeding time (moderate if you are); "Extreme if you are a Draven-Drak." Ignores everything below it except when landing to feed (near-silent descent, swallows prey whole, feeds to need not appetite, then departs). Draven-Drak consistently and deliberately avoid its flight path — a single Kabal restricted-archive record describes the Maur-Rau mid-air disemboweling a Draven-Drak with its talons "with an efficiency the account's author described as practised," then partially feeding and departing — evidence it was originally a purpose-built weapons platform for downing large aerial targets. Encounter protocol: not hunting people; if approaching at low altitude in open terrain, move laterally — it will not alter course for you. Armour likely resistant to standard weapons.
- **Lore/Origin:** The armour dates its active service to before the Great Mage War (before any surviving record) — a war mount for a civilization now entirely forgotten, of which it is the only surviving evidence. Appears to be the last/only one of its kind — no evidence of seeking companionship or a mate. Davin has seen it 4 times in his life.

### Druith
- **Name:** Druith — Lizardfolk Designation: **The Ancient**. Naturalist Classification: Druith — "The Hidden Deep."
- **Description/Appearance:** Not divine, magical, cursed, or summoned in origin — simply an extremely old, extremely large crocodile that has "declined to stop growing and has thus far declined to die." At rest resembles geography (a scale ridge easily mistaken for a submerged embankment). Scales are dense, interlocked, harder than any worked metal known — a blade of "good Thraekor steel" failed to mark it (the blade was damaged instead). Jaws could "comfortably accommodate a small building."
- **Habitat:** Region: Drath Vorrumbruk. Swamp and ocean border territory. Protected fiercely by the local lizardfolk.
- **Behavior/Threat/Combat:** Threat: Extreme if provoked; approach only with explicit lizardfolk escort/permission. Nearly immobile on land; regains full crocodilian mobility/speed in water (competitor only the Zeth-Valdrim, should it ever enter open ocean). Capable of a short (minutes-long) burst of land movement when genuinely threatened — described in elf/lizardfolk war records as "frightening to witness." Scale is impenetrable by any currently known means — during the elf/lizardfolk war, nothing the elven forces deployed damaged it, and its counterattack was "swift and total." Encounter Protocol: never approach without lizardfolk permission/escort; if unescorted, do not enter the water; on land, withdraw to a distance where you're not worth the energy expenditure to pursue.
- **Lore/Origin:** Lizardfolk elders protect it with quasi-religious devotion lacking any coherent theology ("because it is there, and it has always been there"). During the elf/lizardfolk war, elven forces mistakenly targeted it as a "progenitor figure" in a symbolic decisive attack — it failed utterly. Possibly immortal — no record of injury, of it being smaller, or of its origin; still growing at a near-geological pace (confirmed by a lizardfolk elder who recalls it being marginally smaller in her lifetime). Lizardfolk word **Thueln** ("to hold, to endure, to remain") used by the elder to describe it.

### Vaelorn
- **Name:** Vaelorn — Common Tongue: "The Walking Trees," "the Grove Wardens" (no single established name). Naturalist Classification: Vaelorn — "Elder-Watcher."
- **Description/Appearance:** The only Solitary with NO threat assessment attached to the creature itself. In stillness, indistinguishable from a large ancient tree. In motion: root system withdraws with "a sound like the earth exhaling," revealing a tall, extraordinarily thin, entirely bark-covered humanoid figure — overlong limbs, narrow torso, a rounded head as a continuation of the trunk.
- **Habitat:** Region: Vaeloris. Ancient forest interior; grove locations only partially documented. Tends specific groves — establishing clearings, encouraging particular plant/tree growth.
- **Behavior/Threat/Combat:** Threat: None from the creature itself; significant environmental hazard if one has been killed. Never documented to have harmed a person; numerous accounts of it going out of its way to heal them. Communicates via directed, structured non-verbal sounds (creaking wood, branch-groans, structured leaf-susurrus) — possibly a real language humans simply cannot perceive. **Healing**: passive (ambient restorative radiation — wounds close faster, illness recedes, exhaustion lifts just from proximity) and active (deliberately extends a branch-limb to apply concentrated restoration to an injured person). Encounter Protocol: remain calm, move slowly, no aggressive gestures; if it approaches, let it — likely an offer of help.
- **Lore/Origin:** THE CORRUPTED GROVE (critical danger): when a Vaelorn dies, its root system does not die — it corrupts and reverses the healing function into a draining effect that expands outward through the root network, killing everything it touches, and **raising what it kills as undead** anchored to the corpse/root-zone (a "root zombie"/"root skeleton" — see Sul-Daun/Sul-Keth entries). Failed clearing attempts add to the horde rather than reduce it; only destroying the root system itself (not just the undead) ends it — only 3 successful clearings ever recorded. Population history: ancient records suggest as many as 30 Vaelorn; 7 confirmed alive today; 10 confirmed dead (evidenced by corrupted groves); 13 unconfirmed/unaccounted for 150 years (may be dead — meaning an unfound, unclear corrupted grove somewhere — or simply dormant/stationary, indistinguishable from any other tree). Davin has personally encountered 2 confirmed Vaelorn, both of which healed him without him asking.

## II. THE UNNATURAL
*Things "built, raised, assembled, compelled, or that simply refused to stop" — constructs, undead, and conjurations (familiars/summons). Common theme: the creature is often the symptom, not the problem — find and address the maker/cause.*

### Construct (general category)
- **Description:** Not alive — assembled from stone, metal, bone, wood, ceramic (or combinations) and animated via magical working. Incorporating bone (even from multiple long-dead skeletons) does NOT make it undead — it carries no identity, drive, or memory; it's just "building material." No construct incorporates undead material (no known exception). Does not develop sentience — responses that look like judgment/preference are "sophisticated conditional response," not real cognition.
- **Habitat/Creation:** Continental, wherever mages of sufficient skill have worked; heavily concentrated in old ruins as "forgotten constructs" (no living controller, still executing last directive indefinitely). Study path mages are the most prolific general construct creators; Nature path mages primarily create golems; Faith path mages primarily create familiars.
- **Behavior/Threat/Combat:** Threat entirely dependent on type/size/complexity/control status. Uncontrolled constructs cannot be reasoned with, do not tire, question, or reconsider — they simply execute their directive. Determine control status before engaging.
- **Lore/Origin:** The Gods' Hand Kabal permits construct creation only if the creator maintains total control at all times; losing control gets the creator designated "dangerous" with swift consequences. Ownership can be legally transferred via magical contract (including to the Kabal itself upon a mage's retirement/death). Mental cost to maintain scales with size/complexity.

### Thueln-Maur (Golem)
- **Name:** Thueln-Maur — Common Tongue: **Golem**. Naturalist Classification: Thueln-Maur — "Permanence in Motion."
- **Description/Appearance:** Made of natural materials (stone, earth, clay, wood, water, or lightning) animated by Nature path magic — distinct from other constructs because it lasts, since its base materials were "already old" before animation. Variants: **Stone** (most durable, no maintenance needed, functions indefinitely if undamaged); **Earth & Clay** (easier to shape, but vulnerable to being overgrown/encased by moss and root systems over time while still animated but trapped); **Wood** (expressive movement, vulnerable to fire — "kindling that walks" if poorly maintained); **Water** (experimental, requires a containment vessel, usually brass — container breaking ends the golem instantly; philosophical dispute over whether it's "a water golem in brass" or "brass filled with animated water"); **Lightning** (focal points at feet/hands/chest/head, usually linked by metal conductor rods; free-arcing variants with no conductors are more unpredictable/dangerous and draw heavy Kabal regulatory scrutiny).
- **Habitat:** Continental — wherever a Nature path mage has worked; forgotten examples documented across all five nations.
- **Behavior/Threat/Combat:** Threat entirely dependent on size, directive, control status. Can be directed to self-maintain (clean, repair minor damage) but cannot seek new resources on its own initiative.
- **Lore/Origin:** **The Thraekor Excavation**: a sealed general's burial chamber contained 150 golems of varying size/material/function, craftsmanship never successfully replicated since; several are displayed in the Kabal Tower, most deactivated, one or two still showing occasional stuttered activation after centuries. **Flesh Golems** are explicitly NOT legitimate golem-craft — exclusively the work of "Bruised" mages (magical outlaws the Kabal designates as open threats to be killed on sight); Davin refuses to be clinical about them and defers full detail to the "Stitched" (Voran-Daun) entry. **Field note**: Davin encountered a directive-less pyrite golem in outer Sanguivorum, walking an endless meaningless circuit; attempts to bring it under contract triggered aggression and it had to be destroyed.

### Skarr-Daun (Animated Armour)
- **Name:** Skarr-Daun — Common Tongue: **Animated Armour**. Naturalist Classification: Skarr-Daun — "Iron Given Life." Davin states this is his personal favorite entry in the whole chapter.
- **Description/Appearance:** Worked metal armour (not natural material — distinct from golems) animated via Study path enchantment carved directly into the armour itself; the carvings ARE the construct — without them it's just armour. Three tiers: **Tier 1 — The Connected Suit** (single unified metalwork unit, carvings on interior surfaces; a piece knocked loose disrupts the whole animation — cannot adapt, but can hold a position/absorb damage). **Tier 2 — The Segmented Suit** (carvings on each individual piece, connected magically not physically; losing one piece only loses that piece; fluid movement, can adjust tactics mid-fight — "not intelligence... sophisticated conditional response," but functionally similar). **Tier 3 — The Bone-Grafted Suit** (incorporates multi-donor bone — human, dwarven, dragonborn, or elven, physical species properties carrying through — as an internal framework; single-source bone risks unwanted animation from residual identity). Bone acquisition methods: willing donation (ethically accepted), forcible extraction (illegal/risky), or communing for consent (Faith-aligned mages only). Capability of Tier 3 scales directly with the creating mage's skill — can adapt/read engagements/adjust in real time without controller input.
- **Habitat:** Continental — ruins, institutional use, mercenary deployment, private ownership.
- **Behavior/Threat/Combat:** Threat: Tier 1 Moderate, Tier 2 Significant, Tier 3 Extreme if underestimated. Universal vulnerability across all tiers: destroy the enchantment carvings (magical suppression works at sufficient strength; physical destruction works but the threshold rises with tier). **Fire is the universal answer** regardless of tier — sufficient heat distorts the metal's structural composition, which distorts the carvings and fails the enchantment.
- **Lore/Origin:** Mortasha's followers view all Tier 3 construction as necromancy regardless of acquisition method. The Vitae school is a secondary Tier 3 producer; the Fist of the Hand and top mercenary groups use the finest examples. Davin personally owns/maintains 3 Tier 3 Skarr-Daun bodyguards (previously had 4 — one fell during the Sul-Rau engagement documented in that entry).

### Sul-Daun (Zombie)
- **Name:** Sul-Daun — Common Tongue: **Zombie**. Naturalist Classification: Sul-Daun — "Unlife / Something Wrong with the Living."
- **Description/Appearance:** Classified as undead (not construct) because it was once alive. Must retain at least **30% of original flesh** to qualify as Sul-Daun (below that threshold it's reclassified as Sul-Keth, a skeleton).
- **Habitat:** Continental — anywhere necromancy has been practiced, uncleared Vaelorn corrupted groves, or wherever fleshmancy has been applied to the recently dead.
- **Behavior/Threat/Combat:** Threat: Moderate individually, Extreme in numbers (rarely alone). Davin's theory: not the returned/broken will of the actual dead person, but fragments of undifferentiated spiritual material siphoned from "the flowing river" and forced into the vessel — explaining the mindless/erratic aggression. Killing the controlling necromancer ends coordination (a directed horde collapses into an erratic mob) but does NOT stop individual zombies from moving. Variants: **Standard** (baseline, slow, aggressive, durable — dangerous mainly in numbers); **Fresh** (raised via fleshmancy within hours of death, retains meaningful proportion of the original person's physical capability — the most dangerous variant, capability decreases the longer the raising is delayed); **Mummified** (preserved before raising, more durable/absorbs more damage, but notably MORE vulnerable to fire since dried flesh/wrappings sustain combustion); **Bloated** (advanced decomposition, slower/weaker, ruptures release toxic gas — don't strike at close range or in confined/downwind spaces); **Root** (from a corrupted Vaelorn grove — visually marked by bark/moss/root tendrils, anchored to the grove's territory; see Vaelorn entry). Decapitation is most reliable kill method; heavy torso damage can also work but the threshold is higher than expected. Fire effective on all variants except caution needed with Bloated (rupture risk).
- **Lore/Origin:** Creation methods: **Necromancy** — standard raising, illegal almost everywhere (Davin notes an unconfirmed rumor the Vitae school runs a small controlled research program). **Fleshmancy** — "the Fresh Raising," works on the recently dead before spiritual transition completes (a theological argument holds that healing magic is itself a form of fleshmancy). **Root Corruption** — the Vaelorn grove mechanism (see Vaelorn entry above).

### Sul-Rau (Revenant)
- **Name:** Sul-Rau — Common Tongue: **Revenant**. Naturalist Classification: Sul-Rau — "The Witness Denied."
- **Description/Appearance:** What happens when necromancy is performed with total precision — the necromancer captures the exact, correct, original soul (not river fragments) and forces it back before its transition completes. Retains full personality, memory, and cognition of the person in life — but consumed by fury. Circulates a **black fluid** (not blood) through the body, continuously regenerated from what used to be the heart — cannot be drained/exsanguinated.
- **Habitat:** Continental — wherever necromancy has been practiced carelessly or by an insufficiently skilled practitioner.
- **Behavior/Threat/Combat:** Threat: Catastrophic — "If you are on its list, there is no safe distance. If you are in its way, do not resist." Operates at the body's absolute maximum physical capacity with none of the restraint a living mind imposes (no fatigue, no self-limiting) — observed deflecting multiple arrows in flight with an axe. Purpose: hunts and kills the necromancer who raised it, everyone involved in the raising, and expanding rings of associates, until destroyed or forced into the open; also destroys everything else the mage raised (zombies, skeletons — "they share the mage's culpability"). Cannot be reasoned with — hears and understands, but does not care. Cannot be stopped by physical restraint permanently (will retarget the restrainer once free). The raising mage cannot reverse the working. Kill methods: decapitation most reliable/efficient; destruction of the brain or catastrophic structural damage also works; sustained fire degrades it over time; magical suppression at sufficient strength works. Black fluid does not protect against physical destruction.
- **Lore/Origin:** Davin personally engaged one Sul-Rau with a hunting team (his 4 Tier 3 Skarr-Daun included) — came away with 3 of his 4 bodyguards; it deflected arrows, broke a man's bones with a passing kick, fought 4 fighters simultaneously and tracked all of them; the team's champion fighter landed the decapitating blow.

### Sul-Keth (Skeleton)
- **Name:** Sul-Keth — Common Tongue: **Skeleton**. Naturalist Classification: Sul-Keth — "Unlife of Stone / The Wrong-Living Bone."
- **Description/Appearance:** What remains once flesh drops below the 30% threshold defining Sul-Daun. Bone has no shape requirement — a skilled practitioner can build essentially anything from available bone. Makes no sound of its own (no breathing/groaning) — the only sound is loose-bone clattering, which a skilled creator can eliminate entirely, producing a functionally silent construct.
- **Habitat:** Continental — wherever necromancy has been practiced, uncleared Vaelorn corrupted groves, or wherever bone has been available long enough for a skilled practitioner to use.
- **Behavior/Threat/Combat:** Threat: Variable — a simple reanimated skeleton with a weapon is moderate; a fully carved composite skeleton by a skilled practitioner is far more dangerous. **The Sigil**: carvings inscribed on one or more bones anchor the animation (bone alone can't hold it); optimal placement is at the center of mass. Unlike the zombie, requires **active ongoing mental maintenance** from its creator — when the creator dies, the skeleton falls (killing the necromancer kills the skeleton), UNLESS it has been built with additional construct sigils layered alongside the necromantic ones (rare, complex, can persist without a creator). Bone retains its species properties even in death (dragonborn = dense, dwarven = compact, elven = precise); ancient/broken-and-reset bone is more brittle — joints created by breaking bone to fit are the weakest points. Capability scales with completeness of carving; a fully-carved skeleton would be extraordinarily capable (Davin has not personally encountered one, only credible secondhand accounts). **Root Skeleton** variant: from a Vaelorn corrupted grove, identified by bark/root tendrils/organic growth woven through bone, anchored to the grove territory. Encounter protocol: assess before engaging; target the sigil bone (usually center of mass); alternatively eliminate the creator (ends all but construct-hybrid variants); fire works but more slowly than on zombies; listen for the telltale absence of sound.
- **Lore/Origin:** Tied directly to the same necromancy/fleshmancy/Vaelorn-corruption causes as the zombie entry.

### Voran-Daun (The Stitched)
- **Name:** Voran-Daun — Common Tongue: **The Stitched**. Naturalist Classification: Voran-Daun — "Corrupted Blood-Life."
- **Description/Appearance:** A body assembled from multiple bodies/species via fleshmancy, driven by magical circulation from one or more carved "hearts." Ranges from a simple stitched humanoid up to constructions with eight limbs, four heads, and multiple hearts in an oversized torso. Circulates a thicker-than-natural blood that coagulates rapidly at any breach (makes bleeding it out extremely difficult).
- **Habitat:** No fixed region — found wherever a Bruised mage has had time, resources, and isolation to work.
- **Behavior/Threat/Combat:** Threat: Extreme. Exclusively the work of Bruised mages — no sanctioned practitioner or legitimate institution permits it. Hearts (living-donor-derived, can be merged into a composite) must ALL be destroyed to kill it — destroying one only weakens it. May retain fragments of incorporated donors' **muscle memory/trained instinct** (not conscious minds) — a limb may unpredictably execute a combat technique (parry, grab, strike) with no warning, even if that limb has been passive the entire fight. Feels no pain — fire, electricity (causes only momentary seizure, then resumes exactly where it left off), cutting, and acid produce no deterrent effect; a charging Voran-Daun impaled on a spear continues its charge unless the spear destroys a heart on the way through. Encounter Protocol: establish distance immediately, identify/target hearts at center of mass with ranged magical engagement, expect it to keep functioning through damage that would kill anything else, never assume an inactive limb will stay inactive; withdraw if you lack means to destroy the hearts.
- **Lore/Origin:** Materials are whatever a Bruised mage can acquire — corpses, living subjects, intentionally killed/preserved bodies; living donors are specifically required for hearts. Davin encountered 3 in his career, one large specimen found (partially healed/very high quality construction) in a collapsed structure in remote Vaeloris — the people who found it before him did not all survive; he declines to describe it or his conclusions about "what was inside it" in detail.

### Mauth-Sulei (Familiar)
- **Name:** Mauth-Sulei — Common Tongue: **Familiar**. Naturalist Classification: Mauth-Sulei — "Spirit-Soul / The Breath That Remains."
- **Description/Appearance:** A relationship, not strictly a creature — a willing (or dominated) spirit of a creature the mage knew in life, bound to that mage, manifesting as a translucent, semi-see-through echo with faint blue luminescence, preserving exact details (a missing ear, specific feather pattern) as of the moment of death. Visible to anyone. Only creature-spirits qualify — humanoid spirits never serve as familiars.
- **Habitat:** Continental — wherever a mage has bonded with a creature spirit they knew in life.
- **Behavior/Threat/Combat:** Threat: Low in isolation, Moderate in numbers with aggressive intent. Primarily used for surveillance (seeing through its eyes), communication (message-carrying via mental transference), and companionship. Aggressive use causes **cold damage** (deep penetrating chill/frostbite-like) rather than physical harm; multiple familiars acting together can produce genuinely life-threatening cold. Dies immediately when its bonded mage dies (or, if bonded to multiple mages, persists until the last dies).
- **Lore/Origin:** Willing bond requires the spirit to recognize and choose to remain with the mage; a dominated familiar is forced (functions the same but ethically fraught — "the spirit in it did not choose this"). Most mages sustain up to 3 familiars with little strain; the highest documented case is a nature mage ~100 years ago who maintained 20+ simultaneously to tend a grove in the deep Hinterlands. Davin has had two familiars: a hawk he raised and bonded after her death (with him 9 years before dissipating after a near-fatal Sanguivorum encounter), and a hound whose original owner (a miner) died in a Thraekor collapse.

### Mauth-Nauri (Summon)
- **Name:** Mauth-Nauri — Common Tongue: **Summon**. Naturalist Classification: Mauth-Nauri — "Spirit-Beast / The Called Animal."
- **Description/Appearance:** The physical counterpart to the familiar — a fully living, physical creature (bleeds, can be hurt/killed) brought through a magical "gate" that is unique to each mage in appearance (marble, old wood, moving air, etc.) and dissipates the instant the creature fully passes through. A magical **brand** on the creature acts as a location beacon, letting the mage summon it from anywhere.
- **Habitat:** Continental — the creature "lives" wherever it naturally would; the mage can call it from any distance.
- **Behavior/Threat/Combat:** Threat entirely dependent on what species was summoned. Combat is its dominant application (gate-and-creature theatrics favor combat-capable creatures). Maintaining active summons is more magically draining than familiars (3 active summons is significantly harder than 3 familiars). Highest reliably documented case: a faith-based mage maintaining 9 summoned wolves simultaneously — left the mage at their absolute limit but paired with the mage's own rigorous archery training made for "a serious tactical problem."
- **Lore/Origin:** Requires an established bond/relationship (gentle or forced), similar to familiars but with a physical creature and branding instead of spirit-binding. Davin owns no summon (relies on his 3 Tier 3 Skarr-Daun instead) but finds the gate designs the most personally fascinating aspect — recalls one gate that looked exactly like an ordinary house's front door with a brass knocker, whose owner he never learned.

### Mauven-Sulei (Wraith)
- **Name:** Mauven-Sulei — Common Tongue: **Wraith**. Naturalist Classification: Mauven-Sulei — "The Fleeing Soul."
- **Description/Appearance:** Unlike every other undead in this chapter, it is self-made — a spirit that refused to cross "the river" at the threshold of death. Looks like the person it was (face, build, hair, death-moment clothing) in translucent ethereal form with faint blue luminescence; hair moves as if underwater, clothing clings/flows unnaturally. **Both hands permanently cover its face** except during a drain. When revealed, the face is a nightmare contortion frozen in continuous silent-scream agony since the moment of refusal.
- **Habitat:** Continental — found near recent death (crypts, burial halls, graveyards, battlefields) and anywhere living people congregate that it remembers from life (its former home, markets, farms, city streets — it actively hunts these).
- **Behavior/Threat/Combat:** Threat: Extreme alone, manageable with preparation and a second combatant. Believes (impossibly, but genuinely) that draining enough life will let it reclaim its own — the mechanism doesn't actually exist, but it doesn't know or won't accept this. Sound sequence: a building whine (approach) → a scream (target acquired) → an involuntary shriek at dawn followed by disappearance. During a drain: hands leave the face, an ethereal image of the victim's life is pulled out and absorbed; the wraith becomes **fully physical for the drain's duration** (the only combat window) and does not heal damage sustained during it (wounds carry over). The victim is paralyzed (partially or fully) by the drain's intensity and by the sight of the face; only external intervention can break the hold. Victim dies desiccated/hollowed if the drain completes. At dawn it vanishes and reappears the following night directly above its physical body's location — travel range is tied to night length (shorter search radius in short-night seasons). Encounter Protocol: burning the body (its anchor) is fastest/safest — do this before engaging if location is known. If engaging directly: never alone; if you're being drained, don't waste energy fighting the hold (impossible to break yourself), look to your ally rather than the face; if you're the second combatant, strike the head the moment the physical window opens, don't hesitate.
- **Lore/Origin:** Davin's Tier 3 Skarr-Daun served as his secondary combatant in one engagement (cannot be drained or paralyzed) and ended a wraith that had partially drained him; he describes the sensation as "a withdrawal... not painful, deeply wrong."

## III. CONTINENTALS
*Creatures found broadly across the continent rather than confined to one region; entries roughly ordered from most to least commonly encountered.*

### Keth-Rhovael (Stoneback Beetle)
- **Name:** Keth-Rhovael — Common Tongue: **Stoneback Beetle**. Naturalist Classification: Keth-Rhovael — "The Luminous Stone / The Stone That Glows."
- **Description/Appearance:** Urban form: fist-sized, dark-shelled, faintly luminescent at the abdomen. Wild form: can grow dog-sized. Carapace (esp. wild specimens) approaches light-plate armor strength while being much lighter — doesn't dent/corrode, retains faint luminescence after death; molted carapace is more brittle than live-harvested; queen carapace (requires full nest clearance) is the premium armoring material, enough for a breastplate + vambraces per queen.
- **Habitat:** Continental, all five nations; densest populations in Sahrimor (commercial breeding/export trade). Nests near magical concentrations (herb gardens, healer practices, alchemist supplies) in towns; wild colonies form deep underground mounded nests housing hundreds. Regional variants: Thraekor (darker/denser, slower), Vaeloris (largest, dense forest vegetation), Sanguivorum (smallest/fastest, sparse terrain), Norrvael (white, snow-adapted), Sahrimor (uniform tan due to centuries of captive breeding — wild nests rarer there).
- **Behavior/Threat/Combat:** Threat: Low individually; Moderate to Extreme in swarm conditions. Not aggressive unless threatened or swarming; feeds on magically saturated plant matter, producing a luminescent fluid used to extend potion shelf-life/effect duration. Swarms triggered by threat response (faster/more intense) or mating season (more widespread, less directed) — will bite defensively (not carnivorous) but hundreds of simultaneous bites in enclosed space have killed unprepared teams. Reaching the queen requires clearing the nest top-down; smoke is the most effective deterrent, full sealed clothing the minimum preparation.
- **Lore/Origin:** Sahrimori breeders run a purely commercial (non-cultural) captive-colony trade supplying most of the continent's fluid, though it's slightly less potent than wild-harvested fluid.

### Voreth-Sael (Mirrorfish)
- **Name:** Voreth-Sael — Common Tongue: **Mirrorfish**. Naturalist Classification: Voreth-Sael — "The Veiling Water / What the River Hides."
- **Description/Appearance:** An ordinary-looking silver-scaled fish (arm-to-torso length) under normal light; under magical light, the scales interact/reflect it differently from mundane fish scales.
- **Habitat:** Clean, slow-moving freshwater rivers/lakes across all five nations with sufficient magical saturation, mixed into ordinary fish schools. Norrvael populations reflect blue-tinted; Sahrimor populations reflect warm golden; Vaeloris populations most sought after for illusion work.
- **Behavior/Threat/Combat:** Threat: None (they are fish; the habitat is occasionally dangerous). In sufficient school concentration, scales reflect magical workings back at their caster (e.g., an electrical discharge returns a portion — enough for a sharp unpleasant sensation — while leaving the school undamaged; the magic doesn't pass through). Fast, skittish, sensitive to water disturbance — hard to catch (fine nets, patience).
- **Lore/Origin:** Valued in illusion enchantment work since the scales inherently replicate what illusion magic tries to achieve. Scales degrade quickly after death; must be transported in water taken directly from the catch site to slow degradation, making them logistically (not rarity-wise) expensive.

### Rau-Nolveth (Gloomhawk)
- **Name:** Rau-Nolveth — Common Tongue: **Gloomhawk**. Naturalist Classification: Rau-Nolveth — "The Sky-Void / The Absence Above."
- **Description/Appearance:** An eagle-sized raptor.
- **Habitat:** Continental, wherever significant altitude exists; least common in Sahrimor (occasional in its northern tundra reaches). Nests preferentially in areas of naturally low magical saturation (unclear whether it causes or merely prefers this).
- **Behavior/Threat/Combat:** Threat: Low if left alone; Moderate if you enter its territory; "Sustained and creative if you wrong it." Suppresses/dampens magic within a radius roughly equal to a stone's throw — workings cast nearby land with less force, fading with distance. Feathers retain the dampening property after death — used for fletching arrows resistant to magical interference, and for garment lining that gives the wearer a residual anti-detection dampening field (used by both criminals and Sanguivorum Arbitarius/Theoregis/Generalatus officers). Talons used in construct/enchantment sigil-carving work. Remembers faces and holds grudges — a severely wronged bird (mate killed, nest destroyed) will track down and conduct sustained targeted retaliation (theft, dropping objects from altitude with notable accuracy) against the specific responsible individuals, sometimes for days after the offense, well beyond its home territory.
- **Lore/Origin:** Davin carries a scar from a Norrvael highland territorial encounter where he misjudged a shifted boundary; the bird considered brief contact sufficient "communication" and did not escalate to pursuit.

### Dhulorvae (Cave Scorpion)
- **Name:** Dhulorvae — Common Tongue: **Cave Scorpion**. Naturalist Classification: Dhulorvae — "Those Who Carry Harm / The Poison-Bearer's Kind." (Explicitly NOT the same creature as the horse-or-larger **Akharu** of the Black Sands, which is covered in the Sahrimor Regional Chapter, not here — confusing the two is dangerous in both directions.)
- **Description/Appearance:** Does not exceed the length of a human forearm. Regional variants: Thraekor (large pincers, smaller stinger — claws hold, tail finishes), Vaeloris (two stingers delivering simultaneous double venom injection), Norrvael (jet black, unsettling against snow), Sahrimor (camouflaged tan), Sanguivorum (no notable physical distinction beyond coloration).
- **Habitat:** Continental — cave systems, mine shafts, deep ruins (assume presence in any significant underground structure). Sahrimor: burrows under desert sand, surfaces at night. Vaeloris: semi-regular surface population on the forest floor. Thraekor/Norrvael/Sanguivorum: cave-dwelling in practice. Sometimes kept as exotic pets.
- **Behavior/Threat/Combat:** Threat: Low to Moderate individually depending on variant — "the venom is the danger, not the creature." Venom effects by region: Thraekor = deep internal burning pain; Vaeloris = acidic venom burning blood vessels from within (prompt treatment required); Norrvael = partially freezes blood at the sting site (frostbite-like effect, not a coagulant — frozen blood is dead blood); Sahrimor = draws moisture from tissue, visible desiccation at sting site; Sanguivorum = three-stage progression: numbing → paralysis → total body failure.
- **Lore/Origin:** Alchemical/medical applications by region: Thraekor venom sacs amplify heat/fire (mining tunnel-clearing supply); Vaeloris sacs used in antidote development/poison extraction; Norrvael sacs processed into a cold-preservation liquid for perishable transport (cannot touch food directly); Sahrimori sacs used to desiccate infected tissue in field surgery; Sanguivorum sacs used as surgical sedative/numbing agents (standardized by their military medical corps, now used beyond their borders). Davin has been stung once (Thraekor variant, in the dark) and now always carries a light source into caves. The mechanism behind the Norrvael variant's cold-inducing venom remains scientifically unidentified.

### Lorvaun-Nauri (Ridgeback Boar)
- **Name:** Lorvaun-Nauri — Common Tongue: **Ridgeback Boar**. Naturalist Classification: Lorvaun-Nauri — "The Ridged Beast / The Spine Animal."
- **Description/Appearance:** Not a separate species — an ordinary boar with a magical mutation producing a continuous crystalline ridge from tail-tip up the spine, across the brow, and down both tusks. ~1/3 larger than a standard boar. Largest/most intact crystals grow mid-back; tusk crystals are most worn/chipped from daily use (but most valued as trophies/cultural objects, especially by Sahrimori hunters and Drath Vorrumbruk communities). Regional crystal types: Thraekor = obsidian/dark basalt; Vaeloris = green quartz/moss agate; Norrvael = white quartz/pale blue chalcedony; Sahrimor = citrine/desert rose; Sanguivorum = standard quartz/marble. Rare continent-wide variants: diamond (4 confirmed hunts ever) and ruby; one unconfirmed onyx report. The **Sorvaeth** crystal (faint violet-blue, "crushed blueberry" color, most common in Norrvael) is the ONLY naturally occurring gemstone on the continent confirmed to carry genuine intrinsic magical properties — amplifies sigil inscriptions when crushed to dust, boosts potion potency, and functions as a soothing antiseptic in healing use.
- **Habitat:** Continental — wherever regular boar populations exist; uncommon but not rare.
- **Behavior/Threat/Combat:** Threat: Moderate — more dangerous than a standard boar by a meaningful margin. Elder specimens develop **the Somersault Charge** by observing other elders — rolling forward to bring the spine-ridge in as a piercing weapon capable of going through armor and skin; this technique is absent in isolated/captive-raised animals with no elder to learn from, but should be assumed present in any scarred wild adult.
- **Lore/Origin:** Two regular boars can produce a Lorvaun-Nauri; a Lorvaun-Nauri can also produce regular offspring — the mutation is not reliably heritable. Theorized (unconfirmed) to be caused by something in root systems these boars forage on; captive populations lose the mutation within a generation or two.

### Skrevak (Thornhide)
- **Name:** Skrevak — Common Tongue: **Thornhide**. Naturalist Classification: Skrevak — "The Enduring Earth Creature / What Persistence Made Hard."
- **Description/Appearance:** A lizard, plainly — just one that grows very large and develops spike armor with age. Life stages: Juvenile (gecko to bearded-dragon sized, smooth hide, cannibalistic); Adolescent (spikes beginning but soft/unusable, not worth hunting); Adult (6-7 ft, fully hardened spikes — the primary hunting target); Elder (12-14 ft, premium spike material — most hunters wisely avoid engaging). One documented specimen reached ~20 ft, died of natural old age unhunted, and was regarded as a landmark/buried by the nearby settlement when it died.
- **Habitat:** Sahrimor and Thraekor primary; southern Vaeloris secondary; absent from Norrvael and Sanguivorum proper.
- **Behavior/Threat/Combat:** Threat: Low (juvenile) to High (elder) — "the bite is always the thing to worry about." Not venomous — the danger is a severe bacterial load in the mouth that causes rapid, often-lethal infection if untreated (survivable if the wound is cleaned immediately and completely).
- **Lore/Origin:** Spike material uses: juvenile hide for weapon grip-wrapping, juvenile bone crushed/inlaid for weapon durability; adult spikes are the primary commercial harvest (arrowheads, weapon tips); elder spikes are premium tier (more durable, not sharper). An unverified market claim that crushed spike dust has strength-enhancing properties when inhaled is noted with skepticism by Davin (encountered in 3 markets, never verified).

### Voreth-Mauth (Veilwing)
- **Name:** Voreth-Mauth — Common Tongue: **Veilwing**. Naturalist Classification: Voreth-Mauth — "The Veiling Wind / The Mist That Breathes."
- **Description/Appearance:** A large, otherwise ordinary-looking moth — notably cold-adapted despite moths typically preferring warmth (cause unknown). Wing dust glows faint blue when disturbed (most reliable field identifier) — odorless and tasteless otherwise.
- **Habitat:** Norrvael primary; northern Sanguivorum and northern Sahrimor tundra secondary.
- **Behavior/Threat/Combat:** Threat: Low aggression, but Moderate to Extreme depending on proximity — "It will not chase you. It will put you to sleep where you stand if you give it reason to." Wing dust is a dose-response sedative: small amount = drowsiness, moderate = sleep within seconds, heavy exposure = sleep from which the victim does not recover. Used actively/defensively — released toward perceived threats before fleeing. Experienced hunters work in pairs (one collector, one to extract them if they go down).
- **Lore/Origin:** Processed dust combined with other elements produces an "attention enhancement" effect (not intelligence enhancement — access to existing capability, not new capability); the Kabal monitors regular users. **The Legend of the Blue Blade**: a warrior mage some generations past, believing the dust could permanently transfer intelligence into a weapon, hunted Veilwings so extensively he nearly wiped out the Norrvael population; he produced a permanently blue-luminescent greatsword ("the Blue Blade," reportedly still existing somewhere) of uncertain actual efficacy. The species population has since recovered.

### Velthrak (Ashwyrm)
- **Name:** Velthrak — Common Tongue: **Ashwyrm**. Naturalist Classification: Velthrak — "The Ash Creature / Of the Ash."
- **Description/Appearance:** A small serpent, average 4-5 ft (max 6-7 ft), slate/cooling-ash grey; can be nearly invisible when stationary in ash terrain with eyes closed. **Six retractable fangs**, each injecting a distinct venom; all six combined convert the victim's blood to ash (the creature's namesake and food source). A Velthrak born without a complete fang set will slowly starve unless it can access volcanic ash discharge directly, since it can only eat what its venom creates.
- **Habitat:** Thraekor and Sahrimor primary; southern Vaeloris rare; absent from Norrvael and Sanguivorum proper.
- **Behavior/Threat/Combat:** Threat: Extreme — described as "the most lethal thing documented in this tier of the chapter" despite its small size. Bites, withdraws, and watches; produces a consistent, real euphoric sensation (warmth, lightness, comfort) as the victim's body fails; moves in to burrow-feed once the target stops moving. Death window: minutes. A complete six-fang bite without immediate specialist antivenom intervention is fatal; antivenom (prepared for all six compound venoms) requires advance sourcing and is the only field remedy.
- **Lore/Origin:** Sheds 3 times/year (unusually frequent); the natural lubricant secretion released during shedding produces a superior commercial adhesive/binding agent (weapon assembly, armor joinery). Davin has encountered 9 (7 Thraekor, 2 Sahrimor), never bitten, and always carries antivenom (replaced on schedule) when working in Thraekor or Sahrimor.

### Kaeven-Thauln (Plainswolf)
- **Name:** Kaeven-Thauln — Common Tongue: **Plainswolf**. Naturalist Classification: Kaeven-Thauln — "The Pack That Hunts / Those Who Run Together Toward Prey."
- **Description/Appearance:** Larger than a standard wolf, short fur adapted to open plains; coloration black, brown, grey, white, or mixed. The introduced Norrvael population has thicker/longer fur and runs ~25% larger than mainland specimens.
- **Habitat:** Sanguivorum open plains primary; northern Sanguivorum/Thraekor border regions secondary; an established, generations-old introduced population in Norrvael (origin of introduction unknown).
- **Behavior/Threat/Combat:** Threat: Moderate to High depending on pack size; individually manageable, considerably less so in numbers. Packs cap at ~10 animals under a single alpha male; sub-adult males are driven out at maturity, creating a steady population of solitary adult males — a major reason this species is the preferred summon-bond animal for mages working with pack predators (an already-solitary animal is more receptive to a new social bond). Hunts in near-silence with above-average pack coordination (collective prey assessment, route selection, precise timing); does not repeat a failed approach the same way twice. A bonded individual applies judgment within directive parameters rather than simple blind execution.
- **Lore/Origin:** None additional beyond behavior notes above; Davin frames the solitary-male phenomenon as a fortunate biological "accident" perfectly suited to the mage-bonding community, despite not evolving for that purpose.

### Rau-Thauln (The Great Cats — family)
- **Family Name:** Rau-Thauln — Common Tongue: **The Great Cats**. Family Naturalist Classification: Rau-Thauln — "The Great Predators / Those Who Hunt Above All Others." Five related but distinct national variants sharing common ancestry; absent only from Drath Vorrumbruk's deep swamp.
- **Rau-Thauln Voreth (Norrvael variant):** Smallest variant, shoulder height to a dwarf's chest, thick layered white/grey-spotted fur. Assesses dwarves as viable prey consistently enough to be a behavioral pattern. Magical trait: illusion working making it effectively invisible even while moving (only detectable by trained trackers via subtle air distortion around snow).
- **Rau-Thauln Ravek (Thraekor variant):** Smallest mainland variant, wolf-sized. Hunts in packs of 5-7 from interconnected volcanic tunnel networks with unpredictable exits — entering an unmapped tunnel system is "effectively a death sentence." Tawny brown/dark grey coloration.
- **Rau-Thauln Vaelin (Vaeloris variant):** Second largest, shoulder height to mid-chest. Standard variant: green/black/brown forest-camo striping. Deep forest variant: black-on-black with brown undertone (low-light mutation). Both live in the canopy and can shadow a target for extended periods completely undetected.
- **Rau-Thauln Maur (Sahrimor variant):** Shoulder height to human hip, lean/spotted, built for speed. Magical trait: short-range positional shift (teleport-like) up to 20 feet during hunting, with no observable transition — exceeds distances Davin has seen Kabal-trained combat mages achieve with equivalent workings.
- **Rau-Thauln Thaun (Sanguivorum variant):** The largest land predator in this chapter; shoulder height to a tall human's chest, maned, pale-coated, solitary. Projects a **fear aura** that precedes visual contact by several minutes (heightened alertness, accelerated heartbeat, sounds carrying oddly) and does not discriminate by target experience/training. Carries a species-level learned hostility toward humans specifically (from historical hunting pressure) — targets unprotected convoys/isolated travelers/vulnerable groups; Sanguivorum military and Magistratus maintain standing transit protocols.
- **Habitat:** Each variant occupies a specific national range as described above; collectively cover the continent from Norrvael's peaks to Sahrimor's desert.
- **Threat:** Variant-dependent; none should be approached without preparation.
- **Lore/Origin:** A single common ancestor produced five species, each evolving a magical capability precisely suited to its environment (illusion for snow, tunnel-mastery for volcanic rock, canopy-mastery for dense forest, positional-shift for open desert, fear-projection for open plains) — Davin considers this one of the continent's more remarkable natural-history facts.

### Kaluath (River Serpent)
- **Name:** Kaluath — Common Tongue: **River Serpent**. Naturalist Classification: Kaluath — "The Lure / What Draws You Toward False Safety."
- **Description/Appearance:** Large aquatic constrictor, 18-22 ft (20 ft average). Scales produce a shimmering visual "draw" when viewed through/near water — a subtle compulsion effect that pulls observers toward the water's edge without their noticing (works on anyone, more strongly on lower intelligence/divided attention).
- **Habitat:** Vaeloris and Sanguivorum primary; major river systems continent-wide secondary; rare in Thraekor and Norrvael; found only at river edges in Sahrimor. Has been kept in captivity by experienced handlers.
- **Behavior/Threat/Combat:** Threat: Moderate — "dangerous to the unprepared and the inattentive," and dangerous specifically because people dismiss it based on size alone. Once prey is close enough, discharges an electrical shock from the mouth (5-10 ft range) to stun/knock the target unconscious into the water, then finishes them. Encounter protocol: don't stand at a riverbank with divided attention in its habitat; if you notice you're closer to the water than you remember moving, step back; if you feel drawn to look more closely at shimmering scales, look away and don't look back.
- **Lore/Origin:** Shed scales are used in illusion work where the visual-compulsion property can be deliberately isolated and applied. Davin nearly experienced the draw effect firsthand during a southern Vaeloris river crossing while distracted by equipment — noticed he was 4 feet from the bank and moving toward it without deciding to, stepped back in time, and revised his crossing protocols afterward.

### Soru-Daun (Elemental — family)
- **Family Name:** Soru-Daun — Common Tongue: **Elemental**. Family Naturalist Classification: Soru-Daun — "The Path-Life / What the World's Current Becomes When It Takes Form."
- **Description/Appearance:** Not made or born — spontaneously coalesces when magical saturation in a location exceeds what the environment can absorb, taking the character of the dominant local element. Form is always humanoid and consistently androgynous across every type/region.
- **Habitat:** Continental — wherever magical density is sufficient for spontaneous manifestation. Territorial around their high-density birthplace; not aggressive by default — attacks only when their territory is actually harmed (they distinguish exploration from damage).
- **Behavior/Threat/Combat:** Threat: Extreme — documented average expedition success rate is only ~50%. Exceptional, tireless fighters that can instantaneously manufacture any weapon from their element (and switch weapon type mid-attack, between the moment a blow is telegraphed and when it lands) while ALSO casting their element magically at range simultaneously — "fights on both axes at once." Critical vulnerability: must become fully **physical** to cause harm or to heal/reconstitute (cannot do both — reconstitution requires solid form and direct contact with its element) — this is the tactical window; forcing it to choose between healing and fighting is the core strategy.
- **The Dust:** Upon death leaves a uniform magical residue regardless of elemental type. For practitioners: a measured dose gives 48-hour magical capability enhancement; ANY amount over the correct dose kills via simultaneous blood-vessel rupture (no intermediate state). For non-practitioners: produces sustained euphoria and the physical sensation of spellcasting with no actual capability gained. A significant black market exists; the dust appears in supply inventories of every major city Davin has worked.
- **Individual Variants:**
  - **Soru-Daun Nori** (Fire) — Thraekor highlands, Sahrimor desert interior. Contained flame form, heat-resistant, vulnerable to water/extreme cold when physical.
  - **Soru-Daun Keth** (Stone) — Thraekor deep rock, northern Sanguivorum cliffs. Dense, impervious to slashing/piercing, vulnerable to blunt force when physical.
  - **Soru-Daun Velth** (Ash) — Thraekor ash fields. Patient/pervasive, resistant to most physical damage (disperses around impact).
  - **Soru-Daun Vaelin** (Plant) — Vaeloris forest, Sanguivorum grassland. Resists physical damage like living wood; fire is its major vulnerability; reconstitutes rapidly in forest terrain.
  - **Soru-Daun Voreth** (Mist) — Vaeloris swamp, river confluences. Most disorienting variant (distorts perceived depth/distance); high heat disperses it temporarily.
  - **Soru-Daun Sael** (Water) — Norrvael coastal systems, major river networks. Impervious to most physical damage (blades pass through); extreme heat may convert it into a mist elemental instead of killing it.
  - **Soru-Daun Kaul** (Ice) — Norrvael deep winter, northern Sahrimor tundra. Proximity itself is hazardous (cold); fire/extreme heat are the effective counters.
  - **Soru-Daun Mauth** (Wind) — Widest continental distribution (anywhere with sufficient magical density). Nearly uninjurable while incorporeal; brief but very fast vulnerability window.
  - **Soru-Daun Greth** (Storm) — Norrvael coastal highlands, northern Sanguivorum cliffs. Combines wind + electrical discharge (ambient hazard even before combat); most dangerous for collateral damage in enclosed spaces.
  - **Soru-Daun Ravek** (Earth) — Sahrimor deep desert, Vaeloris forest floor. Moves fluidly through soil; resistant to most damage (blows land but don't cause structural harm).
  - **Soru-Daun Saun** (Sand) — Sahrimor open desert exclusively. Most fluid of the physical variants, disperses around impacts — containment rather than direct damage is the effective approach.
- **Lore/Origin:** Davin participated in one elemental expedition (targeting a Soru-Daun Keth in Thraekor) — succeeded, but cost two team members' lives and took far longer than the most experienced estimator predicted; he personally witnessed/experienced the mid-swing weapon-switch tactic.

### Rhovael-Rau (Thunderbird)
- **Name:** Rhovael-Rau — Common Tongue: **Thunderbird**. Naturalist Classification: Rhovael-Rau — "The Luminous Sky / The Light Above."
- **Description/Appearance:** Among the largest flying creatures documented outside the Solitaries chapter; wingspan ~20-25 ft, standing height ~5 ft. Storm-cloud grey plumage with irregular, non-repeating white lightning-like etching on each feather.
- **Habitat:** Coastal and high coastal altitude; Norrvael coastline primary, Sanguivorum coastal regions secondary. Extremely rare — Davin has seen only 2 in his life; slow reproduction, small and declining population (over-hunted historically for its feathers).
- **Behavior/Threat/Combat:** Threat: Low if approached during calm conditions away from the nest; Extreme if approached during storm conditions or near the nest. Generates ambient static electrical discharge as a biological function, building localized thunderstorms that sustain via a repeating cycle; the most reliable field indicator is a persistent, non-drifting localized thunderstorm. When threatened, can directly manipulate lightning within an active storm to strike specific targets (e.g., hunters climbing a cliff face) — has killed hunters who underestimated this. Approach only when no storm is present/building.
- **Lore/Origin:** Not a formally protected species, but population math makes every kill significant. The Gods' Hand Kabal quietly (non-publicly) collects eggs from known nesting sites and incubates them to supplement the wild population. Feathers can be harvested without killing the bird during two safe windows: the brief lull between storms, or the ~full-day window when the bird leaves the nest to hunt — a prepared team can climb, collect shed feathers, and descend before it returns undetected.

### Skrel-Drak (Drake)
- **Name:** Skrel-Drak — Common Tongue: **Drake**. Naturalist Classification: Skrel-Drak — "The Enduring Fire / The Drake That Outlasts." (Explicitly its own creature, NOT simply a smaller Draven-Drak.)
- **Description/Appearance:** Born roughly large-puppy sized; reaches full size in 10-15 years — smallest adults comparable to a large horse, largest to an elephant. Scale coloration correlates more reliably with parentage than in the Draven-Drak (though unexpected coloration/albinism/melanism occurs and does not correlate with capability, temperament, or intelligence). Breath type is also tied to parentage: documented variants include fire (most common continent-wide), ice (most common in Norrvael), acid, lightning, and heated mist (a dense scalding vapor that occupies space rather than projecting — among the more unpleasant to face). Scale color does NOT correlate with breath type.
- **Habitat:** Continental; total population in the low hundreds. Norrvael is a stark exception — Drakes are common/visible there (esp. near Storm Rider garrisons); everywhere else, years can pass between sightings even for someone actively looking (roughly half the non-Norrvael population has seen one at some point, usually briefly/distantly).
- **Behavior/Threat/Combat:** Threat: Variable and highly individual — "treat each encounter as its own assessment." Genuinely reasons, has individual preferences, remembers specific individuals, distinct personality per animal; intelligence grows with age, with the oldest documented specimens (per rider accounts) developing telepathic communication with select individuals they deem worthy. Will share overlapping territory with other Skrel-Drak without necessarily interacting.
- **Lore/Origin:** **The Skrel Trial**: an unstandardized test devised entirely by the individual Skrel-Drak encountered — it decides what the engagement requires (strength, wit, patience, etc.) and chooses its own rider; no two trials are alike, and the animal cannot be compelled into a bond. Norrvael's **Storm Riders** are the primary bonded-rider institution making the species visible nationally. Davin has personally seen Skrel-Drak in the wild 11 times and has a long personal friendship with an (unnamed, by Davin's choice) Norrvael Storm Rider, gained after saving the rider's life during a Veil commission years before the man became a rider — this friendship gave Davin unusually close access, including two instances of the rider's bonded Drake directing what Davin describes as deliberate acknowledgment/awareness toward him.

## Cross-Reference Notes (world-building continuity)

- **The five nations:** Vaeloris (forest), Sahrimor (desert, includes the Black Sands), Thraekor (volcanic), Norrvael (cold/coastal/maritime), Sanguivorum (plains/highlands).
- **Non-nation regions:** Drath Vorrumbruk (lizardfolk swamp/ocean-border territory, home to the Druith); the Hinterlands (mentioned once, home to a record-setting familiar-bonding mage).
- **Institutions:** The Gods' Hand Kabal (magical regulatory/enforcement body, has a "Veil Division" for creature hunting/tracking that Davin belonged to, and a Kabal Tower housing golem relics); the Fist of the Hand (Martialum-trained combat mages); the Vitae school; "Bruised" mages (outlawed practitioners, killed on sight); Sanguivorum's Arbitarius/Theoregis/Generalatus officer ranks and its Magistratus.
- **Magic paths:** Study (precision/construction-oriented — most golems' cousin the Skarr-Daun, and most general constructs), Nature (golems, elemental affinity), Faith (familiars, healing magic, Tier 3 Skarr-Daun bone-consent communion).
- **Historical events:** The Great Mage War (a major past conflict predating current records — the Maur-Rau's armor and active service predate it); the elf/lizardfolk war (mentioned re: the Druith); an incident ~100 years after the Great Mage War involving a Sul-Voran Elder outbreak in a since-erased Sanguivorum city.
- **Recurring theosophy:** the twelve rivers — the dominant religious teaching that a soul passes into one of twelve rivers after death via proper burial rites, referenced in the Orvuin, Sul-Daun, and Sul-Rau entries as the basis for how spiritual material behaves after death.
