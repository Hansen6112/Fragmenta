1e §3.3: move 31 player-owned combat fields off flat state.combat.*
The handoff's own 34-field list flagged burn and creatureObj as possibly
enemy-owned; cross-referencing against PER_ENEMY_FIELDS confirms both are,
plus a third the handoff didn't flag (hamstringApplied) — narrowing the
real move to 31 fields.

Real storage now lives in a private store closed over by a new
wirePlayerFieldsProxy(combat), wired alongside wireActiveEnemyProxy at
startCombat. state.combat.<field> stays a transparent get/set alias on
top of it — same pattern already used for PER_ENEMY_FIELDS — so every
existing call site (migrated or not) keeps working unchanged until step
1h removes the alias.

Verified zero behavior change: full regression suite (348/348) and both
Layer 2 harnesses (boss_sweep, plus3_run) bit-for-bit identical to the
1e-iii baseline. A deliberate sanity-break (dropping writes through the
new alias for nextAttackBonus) was caught by
phase1_step1e_i_player_attack_pipeline_parity.js, confirming the suite
discriminates.

Field ledger note: like the existing PER_ENEMY_FIELDS proxy, this alias
makes the ownership move real but doesn't by itself move the field
ledger's tracked-field count — the ledger's "closed" state is a literal
text-scan for the `combat.<field>` spelling inside step-tracked
functions, and most of these 31 fields are still read/written that way
by small shared helpers (applyEndlessStudy, the checkAvatarOfX family,
applyOpeningReach, ...) called from several 1e-tagged orchestrators.
Rewriting those call sites off the alias spelling is a materially
larger, separate pass better scoped with step 1h's own cleanup (where
the alias is removed entirely) than bundled into this field-ownership
move — confirmed by regenerating the ledger (148/148 open, unchanged).
